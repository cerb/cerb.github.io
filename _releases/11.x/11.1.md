# Identity Management

{% comment %}
* Identity management for portals. There are new 'identity' records that are organized into 'identity pools'. Identities are unique per pool, but can be duplicated between. Customer-facing portals and bots use identities for authentication/profiles rather than contacts. Teams with portals for different brands can decide if they want distinct identity pools or shared ones, in any combination.
{% endcomment %}

Identity management provides authentication and segmentation of public users in portals.

For example, portals and website bots may provide login functionality to authenticate a user before disclosing sensitive account information.

Previously, the Support Center portal used contact records for authentication; but this had several major limitations:

* Portals required contact records. To create a portal that used an alternative login method, a complete login system had to be built from scratch.

* There was no way to segment contacts so only certain users could access a particular branded portal. Similarly, there was no way to create separate identities with the same email address in different portals. This was particularly an issue in environments with multiple brands in a single Cerb instance. A client of one portal/brand was automatically assumed to be a client of all portals/brands.

* A contact record could have an unwieldy number of custom fieldsets when they interacted with multiple portals.

* Contacts could modify their account details which may conflict with changes made by workers.

* A contact can only be associated with a single organization.

Identity management addresses these issues by decoupling identity from the contact list.

Identities are organized into identity pools, and an identity (e.g. email/username) must be unique within the same pool, but may be duplicated between pools. This approach offers a lot of flexibility based on a team's specific needs.

Portals now inherently support identity-based sessions. This makes it very simple to create a new portal that authenticates using an identity pool. Previously this had to be built from scratch.

For instance, a company offering two unrelated products could have a separate identity pool for each product. The support portal for each product would use their own identity pool to register and authenticate users. A different company may offer several related portals that all share the same identity pool.

# Identity Portals

Identity portals. With identity pools we can now act as an OpenID Connect (OIDC) enabled identity provider (IdP). Portals and other scripts can defer to an identity portal to handle login/authentication/registration/verification/recovery/profile functionality. This drastically simplifies tooling. Identity portals themselves can defer to other SAML/OAuth/LDAP/AD providers (e.g. G Suite, Azure AD, GitHub, Salesforce), and auto-import (provision) those identities within an identity pool. Our bots and portals always deal with 'identity' records and have that complexity abstracted away.

# Portal Builder

Portal builder. One of the biggest strengths of Cerb is the fully customizable workspaces/dashboards/profiles. We're bringing this to customer-facing community portals with the 'Portal Builder', which allows arbitrary portal pages/dashboards/widgets to be assembled into new customer-facing tools (e.g. helpdesk, account control panel, discussion forums, comments widget for blog/docs). This is designed how we would have approached Cerb if we started in 2019. We rely on data queries, sheets, and form interactions to do most of the heavy lifting. This is capable of reproducing everything we did in the Support Center in a fully customizable way; as well as rectifying all of the shortcomings of the Support Center. For instance, we could have 'Licenses' and 'Cerb Cloud' pages that show up for logged-in identities with those resources. Those pages could list licenses and subscriptions using a sheet. Clicking into those can display a fully customizable profile page (i.e. whatever information you want to expose to the client). Those profile pages are essentially just dashboards anchored to a specific record. They can use sheets to easily display information, and form interactions to provide actions (e.g. renew, change seats, cancel).
